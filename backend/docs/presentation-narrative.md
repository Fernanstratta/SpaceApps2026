# Starman: a three-minute demonstration

**Opening — 20 seconds**

“From the deserts, volcanoes and geothermal landscapes of Baja California, Earth helps us ask better questions about the Moon and Mars. Starman shows the evidence behind each comparison—and the point where that comparison stops working.”

**Why this region — 35 seconds**

Show Cerro Prieto first. Explain that the strongest local story is water changing sediment, not scenery resembling Mars. Open the competing silica explanations and ask what observation could distinguish them. The comparison is a proposed investigation, not proof of life.

**Three kinds of investigation — 45 seconds**

Switch to the Algodones/Bagnold example. Explicitly say Algodones is in California. Show the wheel-slip experiment and its gravity/material limitations. Switch to the Moon and San Quintín. Explain why the broad word “basalt” cannot certify a lunar regolith simulant.

**What the data really adds — 35 seconds**

Open a location response to show the NASA POWER annual mean, units, grid footprint, modeled status and raw-data provenance. Then show the dated HiRISE product reference. Explain that one is Earth climate context and the other is Martian morphology; Starman does not subtract those unlike quantities to create a percentage.

**The honest ranking — 25 seconds**

Change the mission profile. Scores remain “not yet measured” because paired observations are missing. This is intentional. The system exposes weights, missing dimensions and evidence confidence so a future measured comparison is reproducible. Editorial research order is labeled separately.

**Close — 20 seconds**

“A conventional space page tells you a fact. Starman lets you inspect a claim, test an explanation and see what data would change the answer. Today it offers three traceable investigations. Tomorrow the same API can add calibrated terrain and mineral measurements without changing the frontend contract.”

Do not present the proposed pairs as NASA-endorsed, experimentally validated or complete settlement simulations. The UI is a working MVP; new paired imagery, field samples and terrain analysis are documented follow-up work.
